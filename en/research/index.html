Research

The BRØ1 lab develops a preclinical drug testbed that can evaluate and predict patient-tailored therapies. We differentiate patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs) into target brain cells and brain organoids, profile the pathophysiology of each patient in detail, and combine these data with AI to predict the drug combinations best suited to each patient.

Research Areas

Tech One

Patient-derived stem cells

Disease-in-a-dish models

Starting from each patient's hiPSCs, we differentiate and culture adult-like neurons and brain organoids to reproduce, in the lab, the pathology that arises in that patient's brain cells.

Built on reprogramming, neurodevelopment and stem cell biology, these models are designed to recapitulate the structure and function of mature human brain tissue. They allow us to profile an individual's brain cells in detail and to identify the mechanisms that drive disease.

Tech Two

Patient brain organoids

Brain organoid production

Often called 'mini-brains', brain organoids offer a unique window into the complexity of the human brain, its diseases and potential treatments.

We grow brain organoids from patient-derived cells and record the electrophysiological activity of their neural networks with multi-electrode arrays (MEA).

Neural Pong

Tech Three

Organoid intelligence

Organoid Intelligence (OI)

A recent study showing that mini-brains made of human neurons could learn a simple game (Kagan et al., Neuron, 2021) opened up the possibility of biological computing.

We extend this idea by building neural computers that link human-derived mini-brains to computing systems, and use them as a testbed for patients' cognitive responses. Our aim is a new preclinical platform that can also assess how drugs affect cognitive function.

Tech Four

Digital brain-cell foundation model

AI models of patient brain cells

AI models trained on the cellular and molecular pathology of brain disease allow us to develop predictive treatment strategies that reflect complex disease mechanisms and differences between patients.

By integrating current AI methods such as large language models (LLMs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs), we digitise patients' pathological data and refine models that predict drug response.

Our goal
To digitise patients' cell-level pathology, build an AI-based system for precise drug prediction, and through it set a new standard for the treatment of brain disease.

Methods

Cell imaging and analysis

Using a high-content imaging system (Opera Phenix) and immunocytochemistry (ICC), we quantify the expression and subcellular distribution of proteins such as α-synuclein, MAP2 and TH.

From these images we quantify phenotypes such as cell morphology, mitochondrial function and protein aggregation, which serve as training data for our AI models.
